# Competitor research (internal)

Reviewed in the browser on 18 Nov 2026. Copy, reviews, logos, and phone numbers below are observations, not material to reuse.

## International Driving Agency

Homepage is a white header over a solid navy hero. One destination dropdown, a yellow “Get Started” button, and a row of checkmarks (digital in minutes, money-back). Below that: four icon cards, a photo carousel, year tabs (1 / 2 / 3) with Print + digital versus Digital, an FAQ accordion, a second checker, and a footer that says the company is not AAA and not a government agency.

Strong: the checker sits in the hero, pricing is one decision (years, then two packages), and the footer legal line is short.

Weak: the page repeats the same price blocks, the headline says “International Driving Permit” while the body says translation, and “1+ million customers” and “190 destinations” are unsupported.

## Global License Authority

Dark full-bleed page, utility bar (phone, email, “accepted worldwide”), rental-company logo strip, two country fields, a yes/no licence question, then aggressive lines (“avoid fines”, “drive legally”, “195+ countries”). Reviews are presented as Trustpilot quotes. The footer later admits it is a private translation company.

Strong: issuing country and destination together, eligibility gate, package contrast, order lookup in the header.

Weak: logo wall, countdown-style discounts, and claims that a private file is an official driving credential.

## International Driving Association

Light page, red urgency bar, split hero (headline left, booklet mockup right), two CTAs, then a checker with flags, three illustrated steps, year tabs, destination chips, and a review carousel. The footer disclaimer says the product is a private translation, not an International Driving Permit, not government-issued, and not a right to drive.

Strong: information architecture, flag selectors, step illustrations, destination directory, order lookup.

Weak: the hero promises fines avoided and legal driving in 195 countries, which the footer then retracts. Reviews and rental logos are borrowed social proof.

## What Halden keeps

- Hero with issuing country and destination, then a direct path into the application.
- 1 / 2 / 3 year selector and two packages. Prices stay the existing server table: digital 49 / 55 / 59 USD, print + digital 69 / 75 / 79 USD.
- Three-step explanation, FAQ accordion, destination directory, order lookup, contact, and the legal set already in the app.
- A short legal line near purchase, matching the required notice.

## What Halden does not copy

- Calling the product a permit, IDP, or IDD.
- “Valid in N countries”, “accepted worldwide”, “drive legally”, “avoid fines”, police trust, or rental-company acceptance.
- Third-party logos, invented customer counts, star ratings presented as real, or copied testimonials.
- Fake checkout success. Payment still depends on Stripe.

## Visual direction taken from the gap

Their pages are either a flat navy template or a generic white SaaS layout. Halden uses an editorial travel palette: warm ivory pages, a midnight hero and footer, teal for actions, and a restrained gold only for small labels. Display type is Fraunces; interface type is Outfit. Photography is original (coast road, booklet, card, airport) with no seals and no brand marks.
